## Key decisions & rationale

- **Hess / "Sacher" 1912 → `metadata_only`.** Olga Hess died **1965**, so the joint work
  is under **EU copyright until 2035** even though it is US-public-domain (pub. 1913).
  The policy requires comfort in **both** US and EU for an EU-accessible site → full
  text cannot be public. This is the one pre-1929 candidate blocked by copyright.
- **Vero re dei cucinieri → `metadata_only`** until its publication date is established
  (not in the scan; web points to 1890s). If confirmed 1890s + anonymous, it becomes
  `public_domain_likely`.
- **Forni reprints (Manuale 1832, Trinciante 1593) → flag, not block.** A faithful
  facsimile of a PD work carries no new copyright in the text, but Forni's modern
  added matter (introductions, notes, new typesetting) is protected and **must be
  excluded** from any release. Cleanest path: source/show only the historical pages,
  or obtain an original-edition scan.
- **Dioscoride → link, don't rehost.** The PD work is fine, but the file is an external
  **archive.org** digitisation (Countway Library). Prefer linking to the archive.org
  copy over rehosting, unless its terms are checked. Faithful 2-D reproductions of PD
  works are generally not separately protected (US *Bridgeman*; EU DSM Art. 14).
- **Aloi → `public_domain_likely`, EU unconfirmed.** US-PD (pub. 1898). Aloi's death
  year is unfindable; he published 1881–1903, so EU-PD is very likely but not proven.
  Acceptable to keep `_likely` and not chase further (a known dead end).
- **Cultural-heritage layer is `n/a` for owner scans.** The Italian Cultural Heritage
  Code (reproduction/concession rules) bites when a scan is sourced from an Italian
  state museum/archive/library — not for the owner's own physical copies. Only
  Dioscoride (external US-library digitisation) carries a source-institution flag.
