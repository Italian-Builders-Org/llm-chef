# Editorial Playbook

Status: Draft. Not approved.

## Principle

Historical recipes are not "normalized" into modern recipes. They are interpreted in controlled layers.

## Three-Layer Recipe Model

### Level A: Original Historical Recipe

- original text or faithful transcription
- source doses as written
- language and phrasing preserved where possible
- value: historical, cultural, documentary

### Level B: Guided Editorial Interpretation

- explains unclear terms, gestures, textures, and visual cues
- explains historical units without forcing numeric precision
- provides context for ingredients, tools, and technique
- value: bridge between history and practice

### Level C: Standardized Executable Version

- optional modern recipe
- metric quantities by default
- every intervention declared
- based on source comparison, culinary expertise, or testing
- value: practical cooking, teaching, and demos

## Measurement Policy Draft

- Metric grams/ml should be the main standard for executable versions.
- Historical units should always be preserved in context.
- Anglo-Saxon units can be secondary if relevant to the audience.
- No recipe is required to reach Level C.
- Every dose intervention must be explicitly declared.

## Open Questions

- Who is qualified to approve a Level C executable version?
- Do we need tested recipes before publishing?
- How do we label speculation vs interpretation vs source fact?
- What tone should the public-facing editorial voice use?
